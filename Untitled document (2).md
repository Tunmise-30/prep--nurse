# **PREP NURSE**

### **Product Requirements Document**

**Product:** Prep Nurse  
**Target Users:** ND/HND Nursing Students  
**Primary Market:** Nigerian nursing students, with international nursing knowledge and practice incorporated where relevant  
**Product Type:** Nursing learning, practice and examination-preparation platform

---

# **1\. Product Vision**

**Prep Nurse** is a dedicated learning and examination-preparation platform for ND/HND nursing students.

It will help students move beyond memorising lecture notes by allowing them to:

* Learn nursing subjects and concepts systematically.  
* Understand diseases, conditions, medications and nursing procedures.  
* Apply knowledge to realistic patient situations.  
* Practise using different question formats.  
* Prepare for ND/HND examinations.  
* Identify weak areas and improve them.  
* Monitor their learning progress over time.

The product should bridge the gap between **classroom learning, examination preparation and practical nursing knowledge**.

---

# **2\. Target Users**

The initial product will focus exclusively on:

### **Primary users**

* ND nursing students  
* HND nursing students

The product should accommodate students at different levels of knowledge, from beginners learning a topic for the first time to students revising for examinations.

It should not initially attempt to serve every category of nurse or every nursing examination.

---

# **3\. Core Product Structure**

Prep Nurse will have four major areas:

### **1\. LEARN**

Structured nursing education.

### **2\. PRACTICE**

Questions and clinical application.

### **3\. EXAM PREP**

Focused preparation for ND/HND examinations.

### **4\. PROGRESS**

Performance and learning tracking.

These four areas should work together rather than function as separate products.

---

# **4\. Onboarding**

When a student first joins Prep Nurse, the platform should establish their learning context.

The student selects:

* ND or HND  
* Current level/year  
* Subjects they are currently studying  
* Areas they find difficult  
* Preferred learning goals

The student should also be able to indicate whether they want to study:

**Nigerian-focused**, **internationally informed**, or **both**.

### **Recommendation**

Use **Both** as the default educational philosophy.

The curriculum should remain appropriate for ND/HND students, while relevant topics can explain internationally recognised nursing principles and practices.

This prevents Prep Nurse from becoming either excessively local or disconnected from the realities of nursing education in Nigeria.

---

# **5\. Learn**

## **5.1 Curriculum Structure**

The primary learning structure should be:

**Subject → Topic → Lesson**

However, students should also be able to enter learning through:

* Subjects  
* Topics  
* Diseases/conditions  
* Nursing skills  
* Pharmacology  
* Clinical concepts  
* Search

For example:

**Medical-Surgical Nursing**

→ Cardiovascular disorders

→ Hypertension

→ Understanding hypertension

→ Patient assessment

→ Nursing management

→ Medications

→ Patient education

→ Practice

This provides structure without forcing students to follow only one path.

---

# **6\. Lesson Experience**

Every major lesson should follow a consistent learning pattern:

### **LEARN**

Understand the concept.

### **UNDERSTAND**

Break down the important principles.

### **APPLY**

See how the knowledge is used with an actual patient.

### **PRACTISE**

Answer questions or work through a scenario.

### **REVIEW**

Identify mistakes and reinforce weak areas.

A lesson should not feel like a copied textbook chapter.

Information should be divided into manageable sections with clear headings, summaries and clinically relevant examples.

---

# **7\. What Lessons Should Cover**

Depending on the subject, a lesson can contain:

### **Disease/Condition Topics**

* Definition  
* Causes  
* Risk factors  
* Classification  
* Pathophysiology  
* Signs and symptoms  
* Assessment  
* Diagnostic investigations  
* Medical management  
* Nursing management  
* Medications  
* Complications  
* Patient education  
* Prevention  
* Important nursing considerations

### **Nursing Skills**

* Purpose  
* Indications  
* Contraindications/precautions  
* Equipment  
* Preparation  
* Procedure  
* Infection prevention  
* Patient safety  
* Documentation  
* Possible complications  
* Evaluation

### **Pharmacology**

* Drug class  
* Mechanism/action  
* Indications  
* Dosage principles  
* Routes  
* Side effects  
* Adverse effects  
* Contraindications/precautions  
* Drug interactions  
* Nursing responsibilities  
* Patient education

Medication information should be presented as educational nursing knowledge and should not encourage students to independently prescribe or alter medication for real patients.

---

# **8\. Clinical Application**

One of Prep Nurse's defining features should be its ability to connect theory to patient care.

Instead of only asking:

> "What is hypertension?"

Prep Nurse should also ask:

> "A patient presents with severe headache, blurred vision and a blood pressure of 190/120 mmHg. What should the nurse assess first?"

Or:

> "Which finding requires immediate attention?"

This helps students develop **clinical reasoning**, not just recall.

---

# **9\. Practice**

Practice should contain multiple question formats.

### **Objective Questions**

* Multiple choice  
* Multiple response  
* True/false  
* Matching  
* Fill-in-the-gap where appropriate

### **Clinical Questions**

* Patient scenarios  
* Case studies  
* Prioritisation questions  
* Nursing intervention questions  
* Assessment questions  
* Medication-related questions

### **Interactive Clinical Scenarios**

Students should occasionally be placed inside a simulated patient situation.

For example:

> **Patient:** 65-year-old admitted with heart failure.

The student receives information progressively.

**Patient reports:** difficulty breathing.

**Vital signs:** ...

**Assessment:** ...

Then Prep Nurse asks:

> What should the nurse do next?

The student makes a decision and receives an explanation.

This should become one of the platform's signature learning experiences.

---

# **10\. Question Feedback**

Prep Nurse should never simply tell students:

> "Correct."

or

> "Wrong."

After answering, the student should receive:

* Correct answer  
* Explanation  
* Why the answer is correct  
* Why the other options are incorrect where appropriate  
* Relevant nursing principle  
* Related topic to review

For difficult questions, Prep Nurse should explain the reasoning process.

The goal is:

**Wrong answer → understand the mistake → learn the concept → try again.**

---

# **11\. Practice Modes**

Students should have several ways to practise.

### **Quick Practice**

Short sessions such as 5, 10 or 20 questions.

### **Topic Practice**

Questions specifically from one topic.

### **Subject Practice**

Questions across an entire subject.

### **Weak Area Practice**

Questions automatically generated around topics where the student performs poorly.

### **Mixed Practice**

Questions from multiple subjects.

### **Clinical Practice**

Case-based nursing scenarios.

### **Timed Practice**

Questions under examination conditions.

---

# **12\. Exam Prep**

The Exam Prep section should be distinct from ordinary learning.

It should contain:

### **Mock Examinations**

Students complete examinations under realistic conditions.

### **Past Questions**

Where legally and educationally appropriate, relevant past examination questions can be provided.

### **Subject Tests**

Tests focused on individual subjects.

### **Comprehensive Tests**

Questions drawn from multiple subjects.

### **Timed Tests**

Students practise managing examination time.

### **Examination Review**

After completing a test, students see:

* Score  
* Questions answered correctly  
* Questions missed  
* Topics associated with mistakes  
* Time spent  
* Areas requiring revision

---

# **13\. Adaptive Revision**

Prep Nurse should use the student's performance to recommend what they should study next.

For example:

> **Your performance this week**

> Pharmacology: Strong  
> Anatomy: Good  
> Medical-Surgical Nursing: Needs revision  
> Fluid & Electrolytes: Needs attention

Then:

> **Recommended for you**

> 1. Review Fluid & Electrolytes  
> 2. Practise 10 questions on electrolyte imbalance  
> 3. Complete one clinical scenario  
> 4. Retake the topic assessment

This makes the platform feel like a **personal study companion** rather than a static content library.

---

# **14\. Progress Tracking**

The Progress section should show students meaningful information rather than simply displaying the number of questions they have answered.

It should track:

* Topics completed  
* Subjects studied  
* Questions attempted  
* Accuracy  
* Quiz scores  
* Mock examination scores  
* Strong areas  
* Weak areas  
* Recently studied topics  
* Improvement over time  
* Learning consistency  
* Examination readiness

The student should be able to understand:

**"Where am I now, and what should I work on next?"**

---

# **15\. Learning Streaks and Goals**

Prep Nurse should encourage consistency without turning learning into a game at the expense of serious education.

Students can set goals such as:

* Study for 30 minutes today  
* Complete one topic  
* Answer 20 questions  
* Complete three practice sessions this week  
* Finish a subject before an examination

Progress indicators can reinforce consistency.

---

# **16\. Revision System**

Students should be able to save difficult material.

Features should include:

### **Bookmarks**

Save lessons or questions for later.

### **Review List**

Questions previously answered incorrectly.

### **Difficult Topics**

Topics the student has marked as difficult.

### **Personal Notes**

Students can write their own notes against lessons.

### **Flashcards**

Useful for:

* Drug classes  
* Definitions  
* Signs and symptoms  
* Terminology  
* Laboratory values  
* Nursing principles  
* Anatomy  
* Important clinical facts

---

> "Loop diuretics"

* Lessons

* Flashcards

* Anatomy and Physiology

* Medical-Surgical Nursing  
* Pharmacology

---

# **19\. Professional Nursing Knowledge**

Prep Nurse should not only teach students how to pass an examination.

Lessons should repeatedly connect knowledge to professional nursing practice.

For example:

**Exam concept:**

> Infection control

**Clinical application:**

> When should a nurse perform hand hygiene?

**Professional application:**

> Why does infection prevention matter for patient safety?

This distinction is central to the product.

---

# **20\. International vs Local Knowledge**

Prep Nurse should distinguish between:

### **Local curriculum**

Knowledge and examination preparation relevant to Nigerian ND/HND nursing education.

### **International nursing practice**

Widely accepted nursing principles, clinical reasoning and professional practices.

When there are differences in terminology, guidelines, procedures or practice standards, the platform should clearly identify the context rather than presenting one standard as universally applicable.

This is particularly important for nursing because clinical standards can vary by country and institution.

---

# **21\. Content Quality**

Because Prep Nurse deals with healthcare education, content quality should be treated as a core product feature.

Educational content should be:

* Accurate  
* Evidence-informed  
* Nursing-focused  
* Appropriate to the student's level  
* Clearly written  
* Regularly reviewed  
* Properly referenced where necessary  
* Consistent with applicable nursing education standards

There should be a clear distinction between:

**Educational information** and **individual patient medical advice.**

---

# **22\. Community**

A moderated student community can eventually become part of Prep Nurse.

Students could:

* Discuss difficult topics  
* Ask questions  
* Explain concepts to one another  
* Discuss examination preparation  
* Share study strategies  
* Form study groups

Example:

> **Community**

> "I don't understand Starling forces. Can someone explain it simply?"

Other students can respond, while Prep Nurse can provide an authoritative explanation.

The community should not replace verified educational content.

---

# **23\. Ask Prep Nurse**

A future feature could allow students to ask questions in natural language.

For example:

> "Explain nephrotic syndrome like I'm learning it for the first time."

> "Give me five questions on heart failure."

> "What is the difference between hypovolemic and cardiogenic shock?"

> "Test me on pharmacology."

The response should remain within the student's nursing-learning context and encourage understanding rather than simply supplying examination answers.

---

# **24\. Study Plans**

Students should be able to create a study plan based on:

* Examination date  
* Subjects  
* Available study time  
* Weak areas  
* Desired number of study sessions

Prep Nurse can then suggest a realistic study schedule.

For example:

> **28 days to examination**

> Week 1 — Medical-Surgical Nursing  
> Week 2 — Pharmacology \+ Anatomy  
> Week 3 — Community \+ Mental Health  
> Week 4 — Mixed revision \+ Mock examinations

The student should remain in control of the plan and be able to modify it.

---

# **25\. Motivation System**

Use motivation carefully.

Possible elements:

* Study streaks  
* Achievement badges  
* Milestones  
* Weekly goals  
* Progress celebrations  
* Personal records

Avoid making the product excessively competitive.

A nursing student who scores lower than another student should not feel that Prep Nurse has labelled them as a poor student.

The emphasis should be:

**progress, mastery and consistency.**

# **29\. Diagnostic Assessment**

A new student should optionally be able to take an initial assessment.

The purpose is not to label the student.

It should determine:

* Existing knowledge  
* Strong subjects  
* Weak subjects  
* Topics requiring immediate revision

The result could say:

> **Your starting point**

> You demonstrate strong knowledge in Fundamentals of Nursing.

> You may need additional revision in Pharmacology and Medical-Surgical Nursing.

> Recommended starting topics: ...

This gives Prep Nurse an immediate understanding of how to help the student.

---

# **30\. What Makes Prep Nurse Different**

Prep Nurse should not compete purely on the number of questions available.

Its differentiation should be:

### **Learn → Apply → Practise → Improve**

A student should be able to go from:

> "I don't understand diabetes"

to:

> "I understand diabetes"

to:

> "I can recognise it in a patient"

to:

> "I know the nursing priorities"

to:

> "I can answer examination questions about it."

That is the core product promise.

---

# **33\. Product Principles**

Every feature added to Prep Nurse should satisfy at least one of these principles:

### **1\. Does it help the student understand nursing?**

### **2\. Does it help the student apply nursing knowledge?**

### **3\. Does it help the student prepare for an examination?**

### **4\. Does it help the student identify and correct weaknesses?**

If a feature does none of these, it should probably not be a priority.

---

# **34\. Success Measures**

Prep Nurse should ultimately measure success through learning outcomes, not just downloads or number of registered users.

Important indicators include:

* Lessons completed  
* Practice questions completed  
* Improvement in accuracy  
* Reduction in repeated mistakes  
* Mock examination performance  
* Study consistency  
* Topics mastered  
* Student retention  
* Student satisfaction  
* Self-reported confidence

The most meaningful outcome is:

> **Are students becoming more knowledgeable, more confident and better prepared for their ND/HND examinations and nursing practice?**

---

# **35\. Final Product Concept**

**Prep Nurse** is an ND/HND nursing learning and examination platform built around four pillars:

> 🧠 **LEARN**  
> Understand nursing concepts, diseases, drugs, procedures and clinical care.

> 🩺 **APPLY**  
> Use knowledge in realistic patient situations and clinical scenarios.

> 📝 **PRACTISE**  
> Test yourself through questions, cases, simulations and examinations.

> 📊 **PROGRESS**  
> Understand your strengths, weaknesses and improvement.

The long-term vision is for Prep Nurse to become a student's **personal nursing study companion**—a place where they can learn a concept, practise it, apply it clinically, prepare for their examination and clearly see their progress.
